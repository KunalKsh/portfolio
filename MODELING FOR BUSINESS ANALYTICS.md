**INTRODUCTION TO MULTIPLE REGRESSION**

SIMPLE LINEAR REGRESSION - BIVARIATE-TWO VARIABLES:Y \& X

MULTIPLE LINEAR REGRESSION - MULTIPLE VARIABLES: Y \& X1,X2...

7.2+0.2\*X

INTERCEPT-7.2

SLOPE-0.2

R^2 CAN BE CALCULATED IN THREE WAYS-

1. SQUARE THE CORRELATION COEFFICIENT OF x \& y
2. SQUARE THE CORRELATION COEFFICIENT OF y \& y.
3. R^2= EXPLAINED VARIABILITY IN y/ TOTAL VARIABILITY IN y



USING ANOVA we can calculate the explained variability \& total variability in y

SUM OF SQUARES OF y: SS total = E(y-y.)^2 - TOTAL VARIABILITY

SUM OF SQUARES OF RESIDUAL: SS error = E(e^2i) - UNEXPLAINED VARIABILITY

SUM OF SQUARES OF x: SSS model = SS total - SS error - EXPALINED VARIRABILITY



IN MULTIPLE LINEAR REGRESSION, WE CAN'T CALCULATE R^2 AS THE SQUARE OF THE CORRELATION BETWEEN x \& y BECAUSE WE HAVE MULTIPLE xs.

ADJUSTED R^2, RATIO OF EXPLAINED \& UNEXPLAINED VARIABILITY.



COLLINEARITY BETWEEN EXPLANATORY VARIABLES

TWO PREDICTOR VARIABLES ARE SAID TO BE COLLINEAR WHEN THEY ARE CORRELATED, \& THIS COLLINEARITY COMPLICATES MODEL ESTIMATION,

PREDICTORS ARE ALSO CALLED EXPLANATORY OR INDEPENDENT VARIABLES.

PARSIMONIOUS MODEL- ADDING PREDICTORS THAT ARE ASSOCIATED WITH EACH OTHER TO THE MODEL WILL NOT CONTRIBUTE IN ANYTHING SO WE USE SIMPLE MODEL-PARSIMONIOUS.



ANY VARIABLED ADDED TO THE MODEL R^2 INCREASES. HOWEVER ADDING VARIABLE DOESN'T REALLY PROVIDE ANY NEW INFORMATION, OR IS COMPLETELY UNRELATED, ADJUSTED R^2 DOES NOT INCREASE.



ADJUSTED R^2 = 1-(SS error/SS total \* n-1/n-p-1)

where n is the number of cases and p is the number of predictors(explanatory variables) in the model.

BECAUSE p is never negative, R^2adj will always be smaller than R^2.

R^2 adj applies a penalty for the number of predictors included in the model.



THEREFORE, WE CHOOSE MODELS WITH HIGHER R^2adj over others.
